A tropical cyclone begins over warm ocean water when the atmosphere has the right combination of heat, moisture and wind conditions. Not every tropical disturbance becomes a cyclone, but the process often follows a similar pattern.

## 1. Warm water adds energy

Warm seawater causes more evaporation. The air above the ocean becomes warm and moist, then rises. As the water vapour condenses into clouds, it releases heat into the surrounding air.

## 2. Air flows toward lower pressure

Rising air can help create a region of lower pressure near the surface. Air from nearby areas flows inward, bringing more moisture and supporting additional cloud formation.

## 3. Earth's rotation helps the system spin

The Coriolis effect, caused by Earth's rotation, helps organize the inflowing air into a rotating circulation. This is why tropical cyclones generally do not form right at the equator, where this effect is very weak.

## 4. The storm strengthens—or fades

If the ocean remains warm and wind conditions allow the storm to stay organized, it may intensify. Strong wind shear, cooler water or land interaction can disrupt it.

**In short:** warm water supplies energy, moist air fuels clouds, low pressure draws air inward, and Earth's rotation helps the system develop its spin.
