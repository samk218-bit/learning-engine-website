A reservoir can contain an astonishing amount of water, but a dam is designed to transfer the resulting forces safely into the ground. Its exact design depends on the valley, geology, water level and purpose of the project.

## 1. Water pressure increases with depth

Water pressure is greater near the bottom of a reservoir than near the surface. This means the lower parts of a dam must resist larger horizontal forces.

## 2. Shape and mass help resist the force

A gravity dam uses its own weight to resist the water's push. An arch dam curves upstream and transfers much of the load into strong valley sides. Other dam types use engineered embankments and internal zones to remain stable.

## 3. The foundation matters

Engineers investigate the rock and soil beneath and beside a dam. The foundation must carry loads and help limit unwanted movement, seepage and erosion.

## 4. Spillways manage excess water

A dam must also handle floods. Spillways and outlet structures route water downstream in a controlled way so that water does not simply overtop and erode the dam.

## How is safety checked?

Dams are inspected and monitored for seepage, movement, cracking and changes in pressure. Instrument readings and regular maintenance help engineers identify problems early.

**Key takeaway:** a dam does not rely on one trick. Its shape, foundation, materials, drainage and spillways work together to manage water safely.
